# Channel strategy — who to approach for which occasion

_Written 29 October 2026, from a working session that began with the founder
saying "it's really disheartening not to receive replies. nobody seems
interested at all." This note exists because that was true, and the reason was
sequencing rather than copy, interest or effort._

---

## 1. The diagnosis

Every approach made between August and late October asked a fundraiser, at
their busiest, for time — and offered a platform with no completed favpoll on
it, no other charity, and no live payout. A two-minute brush is the normal
return on that ask from anyone. No wording fixes it, and the silence is not the
idea being rejected: nobody has seen it.

**The plan of record had three legs** — the legal/PPGF determination, the
funeral research and partnership, then favpolls. Leg one is a real blocker and
nothing can hurry it: `legal-brief-the-fork-2026-10.md` states that under
Structure A favpoll may not solicit for a charity with no agreement, and the
platform now refuses to.

**But that blocks the many-charities model, not the first favpoll.** One
favpoll for one charity that has signed the collection agreement is exactly
what Structure A permits. The gate is a signature, not PPGF.

**The flaw is in leg two.** A funeral group will not partner with a platform
that has never run a favpoll. Whichever of Groves, Dernley, Tull or McLaughlin
replies, their second question is "show me one." So the partnership depends on
the proof, not the reverse. The order was research → partnership → proof. The
real order is **research → proof → partnership**.

---

## 2. Occasion, organiser, gatekeeper

The useful question is not "who has opinions about events" but **who makes the
favpoll**, and then who stands next to that person.

| Occasion | Who makes the favpoll | Who you approach | Open now? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Memorial** (wake) | The family, days after a death | Funeral directors (independents first), celebrants, then hospices as endorsement standing behind them | In flight: 4 society InMails, sent 21–27 Oct, none overdue |
| **Celebration** (40th, retirement, leaving do) | Whoever throws it | Nobody. The founder, or someone he knows. Later: venue coordinators, workplace EAs | Yes |
| **Cause** (quiz night, kit fund, hall rebuild) | A committee | Club secretaries, PTA chairs, hall trustees. **They are the organiser** | Yes |
| **Fundraiser** (a run, a walk) | The participant | Charity community fundraising teams, running clubs | Later. JustGiving's home turf, won with exactly this wedge |

**Memorial's organiser is unreachable by design.** You cannot approach the
newly bereaved, so every route runs through a gatekeeper, and
`outreach-notes.md` already records that gatekeepers convert on seeing money
move at a real event. More messages cannot shortcut that.

**Celebration and cause are the only registers whose organiser can be
emailed.** That is where the first completed favpoll comes from, and it is what
makes every memorial gatekeeper answerable.

### THE ORGANISER IS NOT THE RECIPIENT

The rule that decides who to approach. favpoll's recipient is always a
registered charity. A community group without a charity number can **organise**
a favpoll but cannot **receive** from one.

So the first target should be a body that is **both**: a registered charity that
is itself the committee running the occasion. Where the two are different
bodies, the signature has to be chased separately, and if the recipient is a
national charity it goes to a compliance team and takes months.

**This is why Alzheimer's Society (296645) is the wrong candidate for the first
agreement** despite being named in step 1 of
`co-operative-funeral-societies-2026-10-21.md` and being the charity in the
Palins exemplar. Same for St Luke's, Cancer Research UK and the Royal British
Legion. They are all correct eventually and all slow first.

### Stop doing

- **No more hospices, and no more hospice events fundraisers.** Wrong seat
  twice over: a hospice is endorsement behind the funeral director, never the
  front door, and the seat that owns supporter-led and in-memory giving is
  community partnerships, not events. Angela Slack after 22 Nov as already
  planned, and nobody before her.
- **No more cold charity approaches of any kind** until one favpoll has closed
  and settled. See [[project-outreach-wave]]: "someone has already raised money
  for you" is the ask that answers itself, and it needs money to have moved.

## 3. WISTASTON MEMORIAL HALL — the first candidate

**Wistaston Memorial Hall and Community Centre, registered charity 1209180.**
A CIO, registered 18 July 2024. 75 Church Lane, Wistaston, Crewe, CW2 8ER.
Income £138,136 to FYE 31 Dec 2025 against expenditure of £26,572, which looks
like a first-year asset transfer rather than a surplus — do not read it as
cash. Five minutes from the founder's house.

**Why it is the strongest first target found so far:**

1. **It can sign.** A CIO's own trustees decide. This is step 1 of the
   funeral-societies plan — "needs nobody's permission but the charity's" —
   which has sat undone for a month while steps 2 to 4 were worked.
2. **It is organiser, recipient and venue in one body.** Everything that has
   been chased separately.
3. **Sixteen clubs and societies use the hall**, so one agreement reaches
   sixteen organisers: Young at Heart, U3A, Wistaston Jubilee Tennis Club,
   Wistaston MH Bowling Club, Nantwich & District Scottish Society, Crewe Movie

   Society, South Cheshire 'George Formby' Ukulele Society, Crewe & District
   Coin and Medal Society, Crewe Concert Band, South Cheshire Orchestra,
   Wistaston Community News & Events, DanceBasics. Favourite song, favourite
   film, favourite flower — existing topics, natural fits.
4. **It is a memorial.** Built in the shape of a cross in memory of sixteen
   Wistaston men who died in the Second World War, opened 26 March 1949 by the
   Hon. Lady Barlow. The brand statement is the building.
5. **The founder's father has band practice there** (29 Oct), so the family are
   hall users. See the handling rule below.

### Their situation, which is the opportunity

- **Rebuilding the hall.** Plans signed off 17 December 2025; planning
  application **26/0796/FUL** submitted to Cheshire East 2 March 2026,
  validated 16 March. Architect appears to be Bower Edleston.
- **Cost assumption £1,000,000**, from the insurer's (Allied Westminster)
  rebuild cost assessment. A costing assumption, not a budget.
- **The 2024 annual report says "There was no project income and
  expenditure."** A £1m target with nothing raised toward it.
- **The funding plan is a National Lottery Community Fund Stage 1
  application**, written with a paid grant consultant, **Stephen Wright**, and
  **not submittable until planning permission is granted**.

So: a £1m target, nothing raised, and a grant they are not yet allowed to apply
for. They need something to do in the meantime that **demonstrates community
support**, which is also what TNLCF assesses. That is a true reason for them to
care, framed on their problem rather than favpoll's.

### What they do for money now

A **SumUp** "DONATE NOW" button, **bank transfer** (their own page says "will
get 100% of what you give"), and **easyfundraising**. The appeal is the
**Rebuild Fund**. No JustGiving, no GoFundMe, no event-based giving at all.

### The people

Rebuild core team, six hall trustees: **Diane-Heather Edge-Robinson, Bill
Heath, Bob Squirrell, Judy Gratton, Diane Houston, Anne Vetter**. Also named:
**David Price** (construction experience, unpaid input) and **Stephen Wright**
(employed grant consultant). **Diane Houston is named as Co-Chair.** Barbara
Brereton read the names at the 2025 service; Diane Edge-Robinson organised it.

### DIANE HOUSTON — HANDLE AS BACKGROUND, NOT AS A LEVER

The Cancer Research UK Crewe & Nantwich Local Committee meets at this hall on
the first Tuesday of every month, and its public contact address is
`nwncthouston@hotmail.co.uk`. The hall's Co-Chair is Diane Houston. **Probably
the same person**, not confirmed: a hall trustee with a £1m hole who also helps
run a committee that has raised over £1.7m for CRUK.

If so she is the best-placed individual in this whole strategy. But:

- **Never approach her as CRUK to reach the hall, or as the hall to reach
  CRUK.** One identity, one conversation. The same body approached twice from
  different angles is the Debra mistake in a new coat (see
  [[project-st-lukes-prospect]]).
- **Never say the two roles were deduced from an email prefix.** It is
  unsettling and it buys nothing.
- **CRUK's committee cannot sign anything.** Treat their meeting as a room to
  learn in, not a target.

### Remembrance, 8 November 2026

The **77th annual service** falls on Sunday 8 November (the 76th was 9 November
2025). **Collection proceeds go to the Royal British Legion.** That is a
retiring collection at a memorial, five minutes away, and `outreach-notes.md`
names the retiring collection as favpoll's equivalent of JustGiving's paper
sponsorship form.

**It cannot be run this year**: no signed agreement, no solicitor review, and
the RBL is a national charity. **Attend it. Say nothing commercial at it.**
Every later conversation then contains a sentence only an attendee could say —
the same move already agreed for the St Luke's tree festival.

### The approach — route and copy

Published contact is **phone only: 01270 560471** (the site says bookings by
phone, not email) and the site's email is obfuscated behind Cloudflare. There
is a Facebook page, `facebook.com/wistastonmemorialhall`. So this is a call or
a conversation at the service, not an email, unless an address turns up.

Metcalfe shape (`funeral-society-approaches-2026-10-21.md` §"The shape that
works"): one line of identity with the URL, one specific verifiable
observation, a small ask phrased as permission, **no pitch at all**, warmth
specific to them.

> Hello,
>
> I'm Joseph Moffatt, a software engineer living a few minutes from the hall on
> Church Lane. For the past year I've been building favpoll (favpoll.com), an
> early stage platform for charitable giving at occasions that matter to
> people.
>
> I came across the hall's history while reading about the rebuild. I hadn't
> known it was built in the shape of a cross in memory of sixteen men from the
> village, or that the service has run every year since 1949. That's why I'm
> writing to you rather than to anyone else.
>
> I'm not selling anything, and I'm not ready to. I'm trying to understand how
> a hall like yours actually raises money, and whether what I've built would be
> appropriate or useful. Would you mind if I asked you a few questions?
>
> I'll be at the service on the 8th either way.
>
> Joseph

The rebuild is acknowledged without asking about their money. **Do not open on
"you have raised nothing toward your £1m"** — it is in their own report, it is
the reason the conversation is timely, and it is an insult as an opening line.
The last line goes in only if he means it.

### The question they will ask

"Has a solicitor read this agreement?" The answer is no:
`charity-collection-agreement-DRAFT.md` has never been reviewed, and clause 3
still carries the retired "100% of every pledge" claim, which must be settled
before the document goes to anybody. That question is coming wherever the first
approach is made, so the answer is worth deciding before it is asked.

### THE SAFEGUARDING LINE — raise it unprompted

**Never run a pledging favpoll at the Thursday Club or any group session whose
attendees are living with dementia.** Taking card payments from someone with
dementia is exactly what the Code of Fundraising Practice's
vulnerable-circumstances provisions are written about. The right surface is a
**fundraising** event for such a group — a quiz night, a coffee morning, an
awareness-week event — where the guests are carers, families and townspeople.

Saying this first, before being asked, is the most credible possible opening.
"Here is where I don't think it belongs" is a sentence no vendor says, and it
answers the question a decent organiser was being too polite to ask.

**Other reachable local bodies** (from the register and a web sweep): Nantwich
Lions Club, Nantwich & District Riding for the Disabled (a registered charity
in its own right), Nantwich Foodbank, The Friends of Wistaston Church Lane
Academy (1046749, income £2,749), the PCC of St Mary Wistaston (1130599).

## 4a. The music societies route, and the encore (founder, 29 October 2026)

The founder found Crewe Concert Band's own news post about a **MacMillan coffee
afternoon, Saturday 26 September 2026, at Wistaston Memorial Hall**, which
**raised £260.16**. Written up by **Heather Tunstall**. A linked image filename
on that page reads "£614.41 raised" and the page text does not explain it —
**do not quote that figure.** His questions were how they collect, and whether
an **enacted** favpoll would suit: the chosen song played as an encore.

**£260.16 is the first real measurable in this whole file.** It is small enough
that a favpoll could plausibly beat it, at a venue five minutes away, with an
audience of players' families who are already giving. Every other number in
our outreach is derived or assumed.

**How they collect now is unknown** — the post does not say. That is the
Metcalfe-shaped question, answerable from a phone between two other things, and
the answer tells us what favpoll is replacing. Ask it.

### Who can receive, checked against the register mirror

- **Crewe Concert Band is NOT on the charity register.** Organiser, not
  recipient. For context, 50 registered concert bands exist nationally, 35 of
  them Gift Aid registered, average income £12,386 — bands usually do register,
  and this one has not.
- **SOUTH CHESHIRE ORCHESTRA IS: charity 1002015**, CW6 9TG, income £2,059,
  **Gift Aid registered**, stephen@spurstow.com,
  www.southcheshireorchestra.org.uk. **And it is on the hall's own societies
  list.** That makes it **organiser and recipient in one registered body** —
  possibly the cleanest first favpoll available anywhere in this note. At £2k
  annual income, a few hundred pounds matters to them enormously.
- **Crewe Amateur Musicals Society (CAMS), 1212565**, CW2 6JD,
  creweamscheshire@gmail.com. Registered, local, also worth a look.

**The clean shape for Crewe Concert Band: the band organises, the money goes to
the hall's Rebuild Fund.** Organiser, venue and recipient all local, one
signature, and they already run charity concerts in that building. Routing it
to **MacMillan** means a national compliance team and the slow agreement, per
the organiser-is-not-the-recipient rule above.

### The encore is already a settled ruling

[[project-enacted-shape-review]] and the St Luke's note already carry it:
*"enacted option — the winner closes the show — if the act agrees"*, written
for the Robbie & Gary tribute night. **It is a better fit for a concert band
than for a tribute act**, because a band's poll items are pieces they already
know, so the encore is deliverable with no rehearsal risk. A tribute act plays
a fixed set.

### GIFT AID CAUTION ON ENACTED FAVPOLLS — know this before pitching the encore

Question 8 of `legal-brief-the-fork-2026-10.md` already flags it: National
Funding Scheme's published reading is that *"a vote buys something (a say in
the result), so under HMRC's rules it usually doesn't qualify as a donation for
Gift Aid."* **An enacted favpoll strengthens that objection rather than
weakening it**, because the pledge now buys a determinate outcome in the room
rather than only a standing.

This does not stop the favpoll. It may mean **not capturing Gift Aid
declarations on enacted favpolls**, since the charity makes the claim and an
invalid claim is the charity's problem with HMRC, not ours. Decide it before a
band has agreed, not after.

## 4b. U3A — the largest channel in this file, and the technology question

**CREWE AND NANTWICH U3A, registered charity 1073787.** CW2 6SA, income
**£100,142**, **Gift Aid registered**, secretary@candnu3a.org.uk,
candnu3a.org.uk. **It is on Wistaston Memorial Hall's own societies list.**
Organiser, recipient and venue in one registered body.

**The category is the biggest thing in this note: 774 U3A charities on the
register, 567 of them Gift Aid registered, median income £17,952, mean
£29,371.** Each U3A is locally autonomous, so each can sign its own agreement.
Ten within about 25 miles: Sandbach 1105144 (£102,218), Holmes Chapel 1148879
(£100,770), Congleton 1076203 (£39,000), Tarporley 1171370 (£38,042), Winsford
1180682 (£21,503), Madeley 1180332 (£16,524), West Cheshire Rural 1204847
(£15,248), Middlewich 1155022 (£11,912), Northwich 1113103 (£8,548).

**Why this beats the JustGiving pattern** described in `outreach-notes.md`.
There the charity was the gatekeeper holding the participant list, and it had
to be got past. Here each U3A is gatekeeper, organiser **and** recipient at
once. There is nobody to ask but the committee in your own town.

**The demographic is the prize, not the drawback.** U3A members are retired,
meet weekly, have favourite songs, and are the people who will actually be at
the funerals — the memorial register's eventual audience. A first favpoll there
is user research with exactly the right cohort.

**Recipient:** the clean version is the U3A raising for the U3A, or for the
hall's Rebuild Fund. A favpoll for an external charity puts us back in the
two-body problem.

**They will read the agreement properly.** A £100k membership charity run by
retired professionals will include ex-finance and ex-legal people. Harder to
pass, worth more when passed, and they may improve the draft for nothing.

### THE TECHNOLOGY QUESTION (founder, 29 Oct 2026) — the real risk, and favpoll's answer

His question: how many of this demographic use phones and can scan a QR code?
**His instinct is right and the evidence, such as it is, supports the
concern.**

What is findable, with its weaknesses stated:

- **Smartphone ownership at 65+ is roughly two-thirds to four-fifths, and
  sources disagree.** Age UK, citing Ofcom data to end-2024, says **67% of
  65+** use a smartphone against 93% of 55–64. Finder says 88% of 55+ falling
  to **72% of 75+**. Treat the range, not any single figure.
- **No data was found on QR-code scanning by older people at all.** Searched and
  not found. Do not assume post-Covid QR literacy carries over; measure it.
- **Willingness is lower than ownership.** YouGov 2017: only **16% of over-55s**
  said contactless would make them more likely to donate, against 52% of
  18–34s. Nine years old and pre-Covid, so weak evidence, but it points the
  same way. Halifax: over-65s are the group most likely to give **through their
  bank account** (40%). CAF-era figures had cash at 55% of all donations.

**So assume a meaningful fraction of a U3A room cannot or will not pledge from
their own phone.**

**favpoll's answer is one device per VOLUNTEER, not one per guest.** The shared
pot already exists for this shape — it was built so children could take part
without paying ([[project-shared-fund-mandatory]]) — and a roving volunteer
with a tablet, letting someone pick while the pot covers the pledge, needs no
personal phone and keeps the guest's dignity. The live display and the insert
card are already built. **That makes accessibility mostly a staffing answer
rather than a build.**

**But it is not proven, and one constraint may block it.** The guest identity
model allows **one active guest pledge per email per poll**
([[project-guest-identity-model]]). A single volunteer tablet taking several
guests' picks therefore needs either an email each or none at all. **Check this
in the code before promising the roving-tablet shape to anybody.**

**The bias matters more than the lost money.** If only the digitally confident
pledge, the standings skew toward them. For a project whose long-term claim is
a record of human favourites, that is a data-integrity problem, not just
revenue.

**The mechanics were then traced in the code — see
`proxy-pledging-and-the-record-2026-10-29.md`.** Headline: a signed-in
volunteer can already stack pledges with no per-poll limit, so the roving
tablet works today with no build. But the record counts ALLOCATIONS, not
people, so nothing about this fixes the all-time count.

**And this is an argument FOR testing at a U3A, not against it.** If favpoll
works in a room of seventy-year-olds it works anywhere. If it does not, that
needs discovering at a band concert in Wistaston, not after a funeral director
has staked their reputation on it. **The hardest demographic first is the right
test.**

## 5. On advertising

The founder asked whether JustGiving and GoFundMe advertise, and whether that
suggests anything.

**GoFundMe does**: TV spots, paid social on Meta, out-of-home. No reliable spend
figure was obtainable, and nothing about JustGiving's early marketing was
verified in search — treat the JustGiving history in `outreach-notes.md` as our
own note rather than as sourced fact. **The timing is the finding:** GoFundMe's
first brand campaign came years after they were already the default. They
advertised to defend a category they owned.

**Advertising captures demand that already exists.** Nobody searches for
favpoll's category because it does not exist yet, so paid clicks would reach
people with no occasion. What transfers instead:

1. **Every campaign is its own advertisement**, shown by the organiser to their
   whole network at the moment the product is working. A favpoll at a wake with
   sixty guests puts sixty people in front of it, each leaving with a keepsake.
2. **Attach to an existing piece of paper.** JustGiving replaced the sponsorship
   form. favpoll's equivalent is the retiring collection and "donations in lieu
   of flowers". The advertisement is being the thing the funeral director hands
   over.

Both need one completed favpoll first.

## 2026-11-01 — a fourth group: people who already run coin-jar votes

Asked "who has a problem favpoll solves?", the answer that holds up is
whoever already does a worse version by hand: PTAs, school charity
weeks, church fetes and office fundraising days run "vote with your
money" events (a jar per option, most coins wins, total to the cause).
That is the mechanic, done with jam jars. Their pains are the ones
favpoll answers: counting coins, nobody outside the room can give, the
child with nothing is left out, no record afterwards. No gatekeeper, a
date in the calendar, a named organiser. Friction: their options are
often not favourites ("which teacher gets gunged"), so the topic rules
need a ruling before this group is approached at scale.

**First lead: the founder's sister may know a PTA.** The ask to her is an
introduction for a fifteen-minute conversation, not a recommendation.
The conversation is research first (what they ran last year, how the
money was collected and counted, whether anyone outside the room could
give, what the children with no money did, the most annoying part, and
whether the PTA is a registered charity); the seeded favpoll on a phone
comes out only if the coin jars come up unprompted. A registered PTA is
the simplest recipient favpoll has: organiser and charity are the same
people.
