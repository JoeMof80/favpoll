### 1. What this is

favpoll is an online platform where people mark an occasion — a funeral, a
birthday, a fundraiser — by inviting guests to pledge money to a charity the
organiser has chosen. This agreement allows organisers to choose the Charity,
and sets out how favpoll collects those donations and passes them on.

### 2. favpoll collects as the Charity's agent

favpoll collects donations **as agent for the Charity**. Each donation is a
gift from the donor to the Charity and belongs to the Charity from the moment
it is made. favpoll does not own the money at any point, holds it separately
from its own funds, and does not treat it as its own.

### 3. favpoll takes no fee

favpoll deducts **nothing** from donations. 100% of every pledge is passed to
the Charity. There is no platform fee, no commission, no minimum, and no other
deduction of any kind.

Card processing is charged separately to the donor at the point of giving.
favpoll retains none of it. If favpoll ever changes who bears that cost, it
will give the Charity 30 days' written notice before doing so.

### 4. Gift Aid

Where a donor is eligible and makes a Gift Aid declaration, favpoll collects
that declaration on the Charity's behalf in the form HMRC requires, and passes
the donor's name, address and declaration to the Charity with the payment it
relates to.

**The Charity makes the claim.** favpoll does not claim Gift Aid, does not
retain any part of it, and does not act as the Charity's HMRC agent.

### 5. Payment

favpoll pays the Charity by bank transfer to the account in the Schedule,
within **10 working days** of a favpoll closing.

Each payment is accompanied by a statement showing the donations it covers,
the occasion and organiser, and the Gift Aid records for those donations.

### 6. Refunds and chargebacks

If a donation is refunded or charged back after it has been paid over, favpoll
will set the amount against the Charity's next payment, or invoice the Charity
for it if no payment is due within 60 days. favpoll will notify the Charity
promptly in either case.

### 7. Donor data

favpoll passes the Charity each donor's name, email address, donation amount
and date, together with their Gift Aid declaration where one is given. The
Charity is the data controller for that information once it is received, and
will process it in accordance with UK GDPR.

Donors are told at the point of donating that their details will be passed to
the Charity. Where a donor has asked not to be contacted by the Charity,
favpoll will pass only what the Gift Aid claim requires and will mark the
record accordingly.

### 8. The Charity's name and logo

The Charity permits favpoll to display its name, registered number and logo on
favpoll, so that organisers can choose it and donors can see where their money
is going. Either party may ask for a particular use to be changed or removed,
and the other will do so promptly.

### 9. The Charity's obligations

The Charity will:

- keep its bank details and named contact current, and notify favpoll of any
  change in writing;
- notify favpoll promptly if it ceases to be a registered charity, or if its
  registration is suspended or under investigation.

### 10. Term and ending it

This agreement begins on the date above and continues until either party ends
it on **30 days' written notice**. Either party may end it immediately if the
other is in serious breach of it.

Ending this agreement does not affect favpolls already running for the
Charity: favpoll will complete those and pay the proceeds over in the normal
way.

### 11. Liability

Neither party excludes or limits liability for fraud, for death or personal
injury caused by negligence, or for anything else that cannot lawfully be
excluded.

Otherwise, each party's total liability to the other under this agreement is
limited to the total value of donations passed over under it in the twelve
months before the claim arose.

### 12. General

This is the entire agreement between the parties on this subject and replaces
any earlier understanding. It is governed by the law of England and Wales, and
the courts of England and Wales have exclusive jurisdiction. It may be signed
in counterparts, including electronically.

# Notes — for favpoll, not for the charity

Strip everything below this line before sending.

## This needs a solicitor

It is written to be *readable* and signable by a fundraising manager without
legal review on their side — that was the design goal, because anything that
goes to a charity's lawyers costs three weeks. It has not had review on ours,
and it should before it is used. A fundraising agreement is not the place to
save a few hundred pounds.

## Blanks to fill before it goes anywhere

- Registered office (clause header). The company number is in — 11511585,
  from the Fundraising Regulator form. The name is "Josmo Services Limited",
  the Companies House form, NOT "Ltd": a contract should carry the registered
  name exactly.
- Confirm **10 working days** in clause 5 is a promise operations can keep.
  It is deliberately conservative — the product promises disbursement on
  close, and under this model the transfer is manual, so the agreement
  commits to something slower than the product aims for. Do not narrow it
  until payouts are automated.

## The three load-bearing clauses

**Clause 2 (agency)** is what makes the rest work. If favpoll collects as
agent, the donation is a gift from the donor to the Charity, which is what
allows the Charity to claim Gift Aid on it and what keeps the money off
favpoll's balance sheet. If that clause is wrong, Gift Aid fails and favpoll
is holding charitable funds it has no right to.

**Clause 3 (no fee)** is the commercial heart of the pitch and the reason a
charity signs quickly. The first sentence is deliberately absolute — no
minimum, no exceptions — so there is nothing to negotiate.

The second paragraph is there so the model can change without re-papering.
The card fee currently sits with the donor, which is what keeps "100% to
charity" literally true. If favpoll later moves it onto the charity — the
JustGiving model, where the charity receives the donation net of processing —
that is a variation every signed charity would otherwise have to agree to,
and a letter telling them they now get less than promised. The notice clause
turns that into an email. Do not delete it to make the agreement tidier.

**Clause 4 (Gift Aid)** puts the claim with the Charity, who already has the
machinery, the staff and the audit history. MuchLoved's published fees put the
cost of doing that in-house at 3.2% of donation volume. Not doing it is worth
more than it looks.

## Charities Act 1992 particulars

If favpoll counts as a professional fundraiser, s.59 requires a written
agreement containing certain particulars. This draft is built to carry them:
names and addresses of both parties (header), date (header), the objectives
and methods (clause 1), the period and how it ends (clause 10), and
remuneration (clause 3 — nil).

Whether favpoll is a professional fundraiser at all, given it takes nothing,
is the open question. It turns on the remuneration definitions and is worth a
call to the Fundraising Regulator before launch rather than a legal bill —
they take enquiries, and the answer also decides whether a favpoll can publish
before its charity has signed.

## Still to do elsewhere

**The solicitation statement.** If favpoll is a professional fundraiser, s.60
requires a statement to donors about what favpoll is paid. At 0% that
statement is a single sentence, but it has to exist and it belongs in the
guest pledge flow, not here.

**Bank detail verification.** Do not take account details by email alone.
Verify the account name against the charity's registered name — it protects
both sides and, on a first approach, it is the thing that most reassures a
finance team that favpoll is not the fraud they were warned about.

**A public verification page.** Company number, registered office, how the
money flows, how fast it is paid. Point every cold approach at it.
