# Subsets × the pairing table — a revisit, 1 October 2026

> The pairing table (23 Sept), the triad rubric and the fifty seeded
> exemplars were all written at the level of whole topics. Subsets were
> ruled on the evening of the 27th and built on the 28th: 235 of them
> across 106 topics on the local database today. Every row below is a
> PROPOSAL for the founder to tick or cross; nothing in the table or the
> seed has been changed. The rule applied is the table's own: ★ means the
> topic happens AT the occasion, scored on the occasion alone, as a guest
> sees the card. A subset earns a row of its own only where it passes that
> test more cleanly than its parent, or pairs where its parent does not.
> Everywhere else the inheritance rule (a subset with no row inherits its
> parent's) is already right.

Marks: **★→★★** the subset is the constituent the parent only gestured at;
**→★** an unstarred parent becomes a star at the subset; **hop** the
subset sharpens a two-hop row without starring it (worth a row only if
the About wants the sharper word).

## What happens after the ticks

1. The ticked rows in A go into `OCCASION_ROWS`, the ticked rows in B into
   `CHARITY_ROWS`, as `topic: "<subset title>"` rows beside their parents
   (the table already accepts a subset's name; `lib/pairing-table.ts`
   TopicRow).
2. Any new subsets from the gaps are created first, so the rows have
   something to name.
3. The Story engine seed is re-run for the exemplars in C and the set is
   re-ranked, on dev and staging only: the exemplars were removed from
   production (they are not real and must not contribute to the record),
   so there is no production step.

Nothing above touches the generator's copy rules or the home page.

## D. Provision subsets: the enacted candidates

> Founder, 1 October: "some subsets beg for the enacted favpoll use
> case." The split: a MEMENTO subset names a thing the protagonist
> already chose (Wedding song as their first dance, Funeral song, Wedding
> flower, Picture book), and the pick's consequence is the reveal. A
> PROVISION subset names a thing the gathering will consume or do
> together that nobody has fixed yet, so there is nothing to reveal and
> the pick must decide it, or the favpoll is hollow. The table already
> carries the field for this (`enacted` on a TopicRow: "the guests' picks

> group's own"); Reunion's rows use it today. These are the rows that
> would use it next, each with its outcome sentence in the table's own
> grammar, the sentence the closing line promises. This section is the
> concrete set for the enacted-shape review that is already on the
> don't-forget list; none of it goes into the table until that review
> has settled the rules.

> closes BEFORE the night, because the result feeds it; it carries no
> personal note, because nobody has a favourite to reveal; the closing

## E. Counter-intuitive rows: three tests beyond the constituent one

> Founder, 1 October: "is it appropriate to think about favourite pet at
> a pet memorial? it might take away from the occasion." It would. The
> constituent test is necessary and not sufficient at a memorial; a
> second test applies, and Pet is the row that exposed it.

**At a memorial, the topic is something the person loved, never
something the person was.** Two symptoms when a row breaks it:

1. **The reveal is hollow.** If the subject's own answer is the subject
   (favourite pet at a dog's memorial reveals "a dog"), the room learns
   nothing it did not know, and the pledge's payoff is gone.
2. **Difference reads as disloyalty.** The pick at a wake should be
   affectionate in its difference: colour, flower, hymn, poem, where
   picking otherwise takes nothing from the departed. A category the
   departed belonged to makes the other picks a slight.

The About must not be used to rescue such a row ("Max converted her from
cats" would carry Pet, and that is E1′ doing the card's job, the trap the
September note warns against). Rows that pass: Dog breed and Cat breed at
a pet memorial (the reveal is the owner's favourite, which only she could
tell you), Funeral song, Life advice, Hymn, Poem, Flower. Rows to look at
again: Animal ★ at Pet memorial, for the same two symptoms in a milder
form.

Outside memorials the test does not bite: a loud row (Karaoke song at a
birthday) is loud, not tasteless, and the register, not this test, decides
whether loud is wanted.

**Two more failures the second pass found, beside the memorial one:**

- **The reveal is a surprise the day itself will deliver.** Wedding song,
  Wedding flower and Love poem at the wedding: before the day, the
  pledge-then-reveal leaks the first dance, the bouquet and the reading to
  every guest who pledges; after the day, everyone was there. These are
  anniversary rows, where "their song" is genuinely the couple's to tell.
  At the wedding only the provision reading survives (D: the reception
  playlist, the cocktail at the reception).
- **The reveal is the subject, outside memorials too.** Team sport to play
  and the league subsets at a club's own celebration reveal the club. The
  Pet failure, in a louder register. British mountain for an achievement
  is a watch: the reveal must be the achiever's favourite, not the climb.
- **The pick is wrong for the room**, a sibling of the memorial test:
  Funeral song asks guests about their own funeral at a wake; Black tea
  asks them to pick between varieties they cannot tell apart.

**So three tests for the review, beside the constituent one:** the reveal
must be something only the subject could tell you; it must not be a
surprise the occasion itself delivers; and the pick must be one the room
can make without being asked something it does not want to answer.
