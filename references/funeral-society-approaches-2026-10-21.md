## The shape that works, and why

Derived from the founder's own LinkedIn note to Joe Metcalfe (October 2026),
which reached a stranger cold and returned the most consequential intelligence
in the whole PPGF file. Five parts:

1. **One line of identity, with the URL.** "I'm the founder of favpoll
   (favpoll.com), an early stage..." — "early stage" is disarming and true, and
   the link removes all mystery without asking anything.
2. **One specific, verifiable observation** about the recipient. This is the
   whole defence against looking templated, and it has to be something that
   could only come from having looked.
3. **A small ask, phrased as permission.** "I was hoping you wouldn't mind if
   I asked you a few questions?" — not a meeting, not twenty minutes. Questions
   can be answered from a phone, at a desk, between other things.
4. **No pitch at all.** Nothing about the mechanic, the insert card, the fee or
   the numbers.
5. **Warmth at the end**, specific to them rather than to their title.

**The mechanism is that it makes the recipient ask for the pitch.** Nothing is
explained, so there is nothing to reject; and anyone who answers will ask why
you want to know, which is the invitation to describe favpoll. The pitch is
earned rather than delivered uninvited.

**A longer, fuller version of the same approach was drafted and rejected** —
about 1,100 characters, explaining the mechanic, citing the Lovely Gift
partnership and asking for twenty minutes. It spent every piece of material up
front and asked for a diary commitment, which is the harder yes. Keep the short
shape. The material is more useful held back, because by the time it is wanted
the recipient will have said how they handle donations today, so it can be
aimed rather than guessed.

### Never in a first approach

- **"Your name came up as somebody well placed to offer an opinion."** Implies
  a referral that does not exist, invites "came up where?", and is the line
  every recruiter writes. Replace it with the true reason — a named, dated,
  verifiable thing the person said or did.
- **Direct cremation — NARROWED 2026-10-23, see below.** Still never as a
  market-size argument, never as an upsell, and never to an independent whose
  business is attended funerals. But the original reasoning does not survive
  contact with a provider that sells direct cremation itself, and the
  societies all do.
- **PPGF**, by name or implication.
- **Hospice introductions.** A month-three ask at the earliest, and the Hospice
  UK connection is thinner than it first appeared (see the sector map).
- **Any offer to fix their website.** It turns the relationship into a trade
  and makes you their web developer indefinitely.
- **Attachments, links beyond favpoll.com, or a named example.**

### Direct cremation, narrowed (2026-10-23)

The rule was written as "never mention it", on two legs: it is the director's
lowest-margin service, so helping there helps them least; and raising it
"risks sounding like an upsell to the bereaved or a criticism of their
service."

**The second leg fails when the provider sells it.** Co-op Funeralcare lists
direct cremation at £1,445 and launched it under the name **"Cremation
Without Ceremony"** — they named the absence themselves. The societies sell
it too. You cannot be criticising a service a provider promotes under its own
brand.

**The first leg is answerable, but not the way the rule frames it.** A Head of
Funeral is not weighing the margin on one cremation. The worry is that the
fastest-growing fifth of the market is a product with **no relationship in
it**: an attended funeral leaves them a room full of people, an order of
service carrying their name, and a family who remembers them; a direct
cremation leaves a transaction. And every distribution channel in the sector
map — the insert card, the order of service, the live screen — is absent from
precisely that segment.

**So it becomes a QUESTION, not a pitch, which is the shape this file already
argues for.** *When a family takes a direct cremation, is there anything you
can offer the people who would have come?* Only they can answer it, it costs
nothing to ask, and it invites them to reach for the thing rather than be
sold it.

**Keep one honesty.** A family that chose a direct cremation chose less
ceremony — sometimes for cost, sometimes because the person wanted no fuss.
A participation mechanic can read as reintroducing what they declined.
`ppgf-volume-note-2026-10.md` says that is unsizable in either direction and
it still is, which is exactly why it is asked rather than asserted.

## 1. Glenn Barsby, OurCoop / FuneralCoop — **SENT 21 October 2026**

Head of Funeral, FuneralCoop / OurCoop Group. 150+ funeral homes, 16,966
arrangements last year. Sent as a **LinkedIn InMail** (Premium Business, 15
credits — the credit is refunded if he replies within 90 days). His profile
confirms the title as **Head of Funeral**.

**Subject, as sent:** Seeking insight into in-memory charitable donations

Two earlier subjects were rejected, and why:

- *"Fundraising platform for funerals"* — a product description, indistinguishable
  from the vendor InMails he already deletes, and it pulled against the body:
  the subject said *I have a product for your sector* while the message said
  *I'd value your view*.
- *"Your Dying Matters comments, and a question"* — mine, rejected by the
  founder as **too personal**, and he was right: referencing something the
  recipient said, in the subject, from a stranger, has a knowing quality that
  is wrong in this setting.

The one that worked names **his subject matter**, not him and not the product.
"Seeking insight" mirrors the body's own register, and **"charitable"** removes
a real ambiguity — "in-memory" alone can read as a computing term.

**The founder's own copy, final:**

> Hi Glenn,
>
> I'm the founder of favpoll (favpoll.com), an early stage fundraising platform
> designed for use at memorial services and funerals.
>
> Before launching properly, I'm trying to understand more about in-memory
> donations and how I can refine favpoll to add the most value.
>
> I saw your comments for Dying Matters Awareness Week, which is why your name
> came to mind. I was hoping you wouldn't mind if I asked you a few questions?
>
> Thanks very much in advance.
>
> Kind Regards,
>
> Joe Moffatt

**Why this version and not the earlier ones.** Three drafts were discarded, and
what each one got wrong is the useful part:

1. **Mine, ~1,100 characters** — explained the mechanic, cited the Lovely Gift
   partnership, asked for twenty minutes. Spent every piece of material up
   front and asked for a diary commitment.
2. **"Appropriate, ethical and useful"** — the founder's proven Palins line,
   but **too vague for this reader**. "A few questions about whether my
   platform is ethical" is an unbounded favour, not a shared interest, and a
   busy person's default answer to an unbounded favour is no reply. Joe
   Metcalfe replied because PPGF was a concrete mutual topic. Glenn needed one
   too, and it is **in-memory donations** — his operational reality and
   favpoll's whole subject.
3. **Also, "your name came up as somebody well placed to offer an opinion"** —
   implied a referral that does not exist and is the line every recruiter
   writes. Replaced with the true reason.

**"How I can refine favpoll to add the most value" is load-bearing.** It states
the commercial interest openly, which is what stops the approach being a
request for an opinion followed by a pitch. The founder raised that concern
himself; the answer was not to disclose a future pitch but to stop claiming
the opinion was the only purpose. Note what is *absent*: no promise not to sell.
An earlier draft of mine said "I am not asking for business", which would have
been the actually dishonest line.

**The link is favpoll.com, not /memorial.** `rotating-register-hero.tsx` opens
on the **memorial** slide with a 19-second dwell before it crossfades, so a
funeral professional lands on the right register anyway and then sees the
breadth — which is what makes favpoll look like a platform rather than a
single-purpose funeral tool. `/memorial` has a different job: it is the QR
destination on co-branded stationery, where the reader is a bereaved family who
must not land on celebration copy.

### The observation, sourced — and he did not write it

The piece is **Membership Matters, 5 May 2026**, headlined *"We wish we'd
talked about it": Families urged to discuss funeral wishes*, credited to the
publication with no named author. **Barsby is quoted in it, not its author** —
no piece authored by him exists that could be found, which is why the message
says "your comments" and not "your piece". Getting that wrong would have
undermined the one sentence whose job is proving the approach is not templated.
<https://www.membershipmatters.coop/we-wish-wed-talked-about-it-families-urged-to-discuss-funeral-wishes/>

His words in full:

> *"These are some of the most important conversations we can have, yet they're
> often the ones we put off the longest. What we're hearing is that people want
> to talk, they just don't know how to start. And when the moment passes, that
> uncertainty can turn into regret."*

### THAT QUOTE LEADS STRAIGHT TO THE WILL-DIRECTED FAVPOLL — hold it for the call

The founder's observation, 21 October 2026, and it is the strongest bridge in
the whole approach because **the problem Glenn states is one favpoll answers**:

- *"They just don't know how to start."* A favourite is a way to start.
  "What was your favourite song?" gets answered at a kitchen table where "have
  you thought about your funeral?" gets shut down.
- *"When the moment passes, that uncertainty can turn into regret."* The
  will-directed favpoll is exactly the thing that catches it before the moment
  passes — the person records their own favourites and their own reveal in
  advance, in the first person, and the family hears their voice afterwards.

So favpoll's resonance with Glenn is **not the collection at all** — it is
pre-need, and it connects to the funeral-plan offer and the letter-of-wishes
kit.

**Two guards.** First, **do not overclaim**: favpoll captures favourites, not
wishes. It is a **doorway** into the conversation his campaign is about, not
the conversation itself, and the letter of wishes remains the actual artefact.
The modest version is true and therefore credible; the grand version is neither.
Second, **do not lead with it.** A pre-paid funeral plan is an FCA-regulated
promise and favpoll cannot guarantee a feature twenty-five years out, so this
is a second- or third-conversation idea that has to sit alongside a plan rather
than inside it. See
`references/co-operative-funeral-societies-2026-10-21.md` section 5.

Also in that article, worth knowing before the call: **they ran their own
members survey** on this (people are willing to discuss funeral wishes but do
not know how to start; the bereaved wish they had talked sooner), and
**FuneralCoop funds free family support with no revenue attached** — monthly
grief coffee mornings and **GriefChat**, a free bereavement service, Monday to
Friday 9am–9pm. That second one is a better precedent than Lovely Gift for a
free member-facing favpoll: they already pay third parties to serve families
where there is no margin.

## 2. The second message — when he asks what favpoll is

Held back from the first approach on purpose. Send when invited.

> favpoll gives the people at a funeral a way to take part in the collection
> instead of passing a basket. The family picks a topic that meant something, a
> favourite song or a favourite place. Each guest picks their own favourite and
> pledges what it is worth to them. The money goes to a registered charity the
> family names, and favpoll takes no fee from a pledge.
>
> It is built to sit with the order of service rather than to replace anything.
>
> I noticed FuneralCoop's partnership with The Lovely Gift Group, so a partner
> in that space will not be a new idea to you.
>
> What I would value is your judgement on whether this is appropriate in
> practice, before it goes anywhere near a real family. Twenty minutes,
> whenever suits — I will work around you.

**Still held back even here:** the £802k comparison, the insert card and the
live screen. Those wait until he has said how FuneralCoop handles in-memory
donations today, so they can be aimed rather than guessed.

## Reusing this for the rest of the society tier

Three more are worth an approach, in this order:

| Society | Homes | Est. funerals/yr |
|---|---|---|
| East of England Co-op | ~60 | ~6,000–6,800 |
| Scotmid | 40 | ~4,000–4,500 |
| Lincolnshire Co-op | ~20 | ~2,000–2,300 |

**Each needs its own opening observation.** A visibly reused message is worse
than none: the sector is small, these people know each other, and the
observation is the only thing separating this from the outreach they delete.
Ten minutes per message finding something real is the whole cost.

Leave **Southern Co-op** alone until the CMA rules — nobody there can sign
anything this year. **Heart of England** (~6 homes) is a free connection
request, not an InMail.

### The channel, and the clock

The founder holds **LinkedIn Premium for one month from about 21 October 2026**.
That matters more than any single message:

- **InMail reaches someone without needing a connection accept**, and gives
  ~1,900 characters against a connection request's 300. It also removes the
  silent-failure risk of an inferred email address.
- **Credits:** Premium Career ~5/month, Business ~15 — check the balance in the
  Premium hub. A credit is **refunded if the recipient replies within 90 days**,
  so a message that lands is effectively free. Assume they die with the
  subscription: use them inside the month rather than hoarding them.
- **Premium search is also how to find the three names above**, which was
  otherwise a research task.
- **Send in week one.** Do not wait out connection requests first; the month is
  too short for a five-day accept cycle. Free connection requests cost nothing,
  so send those in parallel to everyone regardless.
- **The profile is the landing page.** The message asks the recipient to judge
  whether favpoll is appropriate; the first thing they will do is click the
  name. Worth checking the headline says software engineer building favpoll.
- **Watch profile views.** A view with no reply means it landed and they are
  deciding — a reason to follow up once, not to read it as a no.
