## What PPGF's framing requires

From PPGF UK's own published terms:

> "All donations through PayPal Giving Fund programmes are **gifts made
> voluntarily to PayPal Giving Fund**, a registered charity in England and
> Wales (No. 1110538), **for the benefit of a charity chosen by the donor**."
>
> "PayPal Giving Fund has **legal control over all donations**."

And from Thrift+, a UK partner, in their own terms:

> "we make a donation on your behalf to PayPal Giving Fund (registered charity
> No. 1110538)" — which then "makes a grant to your **Suggested Charity**".

**The vocabulary is the architecture.** The donor *suggests*; PPGF *grants*;
PPGF holds legal control. That is what makes PPGF the recipient — and, we
believe, our counterparty for the purposes of Part II of the Charities Act
1992, which is the question we are putting to our own solicitor rather than
assuming.

## 1. `/terms` — "Pledges and money", first paragraph

**Now:**

> A pledge is a gift to the favpoll's chosen registered charity — never to the
> organiser, and never to favpoll.

**Proposed:**

> A pledge is a gift to PayPal Giving Fund, a registered charity in England and
> Wales (no. 1110538), for the benefit of the charity this favpoll names. It is
> never a gift to the organiser, and never to favpoll.
>
> PayPal Giving Fund grants the money on to that charity, usually within 15 to
> 45 days of receiving it. In the rare case that it cannot — if a charity has
> left the register, say — it will ask for another to be recommended, and may
> grant to a similar charity instead. If that ever happens on a favpoll, we
> will tell the organiser, ask them to choose again, and say plainly what
> became of the money.

The second paragraph is ours, not theirs, and we would like to keep it: the
discretion clause is the single thing a bereaved family would most want
explained, and burying it would be the wrong instinct.

## 2. The donation page — a disclosure that does not exist yet

There is currently no sentence at the point of pledging that says who receives
the money; the itemised total shows a line reading **"To &lt;charity&gt;"** and
nothing more. Under PPGF's framing that line is itself a representation, and
an unqualified one.

**Proposed, beneath the itemised total:**

> Your pledge goes to PayPal Giving Fund, a registered charity, which grants it
> to **&lt;charity&gt;**. favpoll takes nothing from it.

And the breakdown line changes from `To <charity>` to `For <charity>` — one
word, and the only one that is accurate once PPGF is the recipient.

## 3. What stays exactly as it is

- **"Pledge"** is ours and survives. It is not a donation-industry word and
  that is the point.
- **The no-fee promise** — "favpoll charges no platform fee and takes nothing
  from your pledge" — is unaffected and true under either model.
- **The retired claim stays retired.** Nothing here reinstates "100% of every
  pledge reaches the charity". Processing is still met from the pledge.
- **The optional contribution** stays a separate line, itemised before payment,
  with "None" first. It is favpoll's income and never the charity's money, and
  nothing about PPGF changes that.

## What this costs us — the part worth arguing about

**"Suggested" is weaker than what favpoll currently promises, and at a funeral
that is not a small difference.**

Today a family is told the money goes to the hospice that cared for their
mother. Under PPGF's framing they are told it is a gift to PayPal Giving Fund
for the benefit of the hospice they suggest, which PPGF will grant on and might
— rarely — grant elsewhere. Both sentences are honest. Only one of them is the
sentence a grieving family wants to read.

That is the real price of this model, and it is not a wording problem to be
written around. It is a thing we are choosing.

**Three reasons to pay it anyway:**

1. **The alternative is worse for the same family.** Without a rail, the hospice
   gets nothing at all — which is today's position, 42 charities pending and no
   pledge button.
2. **The discretion is rare and we have an answer to it.** The founder's own
   ruling, 2026-10-13: *"even in memorial cases, i think it is forgivable to try
   and fail to donate, notifying the family and requesting an alternative.
   Surely they wouldn't mind and if they did, favpoll could match the
   donation."* Handled openly it is a service recovery, not a scandal.
3. **It buys the register.** One PPGF contract and we can name any registered
   charity, rather than only those we have personally onboarded. The small local
   hospice becomes reachable — and the small local hospice is the whole case.

**What we should not do** is hide the change in small print to keep the warmer
sentence. If the wording is the price of the rail, it goes where a guest will
read it.
