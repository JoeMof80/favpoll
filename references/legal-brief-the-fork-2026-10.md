## 1. Who we are

**Josmo Services Limited** (company 11511585), trading as **favpoll**
(favpoll.com). A UK platform for charitable giving at life's significant
moments — memorials, celebrations, retirements.

An organiser creates a "favpoll" for an occasion and nominates up to three
registered charities. Guests pledge a small amount (typically £2–£20), pick a
favourite from a poll of someone's favourite things, and the proceeds go to
the nominated charities.

**Money:** favpoll charges **no platform fee and takes nothing from a pledge**.
Card processing (Stripe, 1.5% + 20p on UK domestic cards) is absorbed at
settlement, so a charity receives the pledge less its share of processing. Our
only revenue is **optional contributions** guests may add for favpoll,
collected separately from the donation.

**Status, stated plainly: we are not yet operating.** The site has been live
since 7 September 2026, but the only favpolls on it are our own tests and the
only transaction is a 96p test donation. **No money has been raised for any
charity, and no charity has agreed anything with us.** We are asking these
questions before we begin, not after.

One qualification we would rather raise than have found: until 12 October the
test favpolls were publicly reachable and named charities that had not agreed
to receive anything. They are now unlisted, every charity is marked pending,
and the platform refuses to take money for any charity that has not agreed.

**What the regulator has already said.** The Fundraising Regulator (1 September
2026) confirmed it cannot rule on professional fundraiser / commercial
participator status — that turns on our own legal advice — and that **Code of
Fundraising Practice 6.2.1 expects a written agreement between a charity and a
third-party fundraiser regardless of the statutory answer**. It also confirmed
non-charity registration with the Regulator is open to us.

## 2. The two structures

**Structure A — direct remittance.** favpoll collects pledges through its own
Stripe account, holds the funds briefly, and remits to each charity's own bank
account under a written agreement with that charity. favpoll would collect **as
agent** for the charity. Gift Aid declarations are captured by favpoll and
passed to the charity to claim; favpoll claims nothing and takes nothing from
Gift Aid. This is approximately the JustGiving model, and requires every
charity to be onboarded before it can receive anything.

**Structure B — charitable intermediary.** The donation goes to a registered
charitable intermediary — PayPal Giving Fund UK (charity 1110538) is the
candidate; GoFundMe's UK charity fundraisers use it — and the donor
**recommends** a beneficiary charity. The intermediary retains discretion,
grants the funds onward, and claims and passes on Gift Aid itself. favpoll may
either route donors through the intermediary's checkout, or collect and grant
the proceeds onward with per-charity recommendations. This reaches charities
that have no relationship with us at all, which the direct model structurally
cannot.

Structure B is not secured. An approach is in progress and may fail.

## 3. What we are asking

**Under each structure, separately:**

1. Is favpoll a **professional fundraiser** and/or a **commercial participator**
   under Part II of the Charities Act 1992 and the 1994 Regulations?

2. If a written agreement is required under **s.59**, **with whom**? Under A we
   assume each beneficiary charity. Under B, is the agreement with the
   intermediary — the institution actually receiving the gift — or still with
   each charity the donor recommends?

3. What **solicitation statement** does s.60 require, and in what words? Note
   we take nothing from a pledge, so we expect this to be favourable to us;
   we want it accurate rather than flattering.

4. May we solicit donations **naming a charity that has no agreement with us**?
   This is the commercially decisive question. Our understanding is no under A,
   and possibly yes under B, since the gift is to the intermediary.

5. Under A, favpoll holds charitable funds between pledge and remittance. Does
   that create any regulated activity, client-money or safeguarding obligation,
   and does the **agent rather than principal** characterisation hold? We
   intend the pledges never to be favpoll's turnover.

6. Under B, can a grant carry an **attribution** — "in memory of …"? Attribution
   matters unusually much in our context and may be decisive for whether B is
   viable for us at all.

7. **Under B, what must we tell guests about the intermediary's discretion?**
   PayPal Giving Fund's own disclosure says that where it cannot fund the
   recommended charity it will ask for another recommendation, and *"grant the
   funds to a similar charity if you don't respond"*. That discretion is what
   keeps the Gift Aid valid, so we do not want it removed — but it means money
   given in a named person's memory, for a named charity, may reach a
   different one. Our guests are often at a funeral. What must be said to
   them, and when, for that to be a fair representation rather than a
   misdescription?

**And on Gift Aid, which may not depend on the structure at all:**

8. **Does a pledge qualify for Gift Aid?** We currently capture declarations
   and pass them to the charity to claim; we claim nothing and take nothing
   from Gift Aid. Two features give us pause:

   - a guest's pledge is allocated to the favourite they pick, and the
     standings on a favpoll are the **sum of pledges** per favourite — so a
     larger pledge moves the result more; and
   - an optional **personal note**, written in advance by or about the person
     being honoured, is shown to a guest only after they have pledged.

   National Funding Scheme, a registered charity running a pay-per-vote
   fundraising product, publishes this reading: *"A vote buys something (a say
   in the result), so under HMRC's rules it usually doesn't qualify as a
   donation for Gift Aid."* Our pledge differs — any amount, one pick per
   guest, and a note with no market value — but we would rather be told than
   assume. **If these are benefits that disqualify, we must stop capturing
   declarations immediately**, because the claim is made by the charity, and
   an invalid claim is the charity's problem with HMRC rather than ours.

   If it does disqualify, we would want to know which feature causes it: the
   money-weighted standing is a design dial we could change; the note is the
   product.

   Related: a guest may pledge from a communal "shared pot" topped up by other
   guests, so the person who paid and the person who receives the note can be
   different people, and the pot is pooled and anonymous. We do not know
   whether that distinction helps.

**And independently of all of it — these do not wait on anything:**

9. **Terms of service.** We have none at all. The site is live with card
   payments integrated and will take them the moment we begin.
10. **A review of our privacy policy**, which is live.
