## 3. CAF — first contact

**Revised 26 Aug after checking the product range.** The first draft asked
about coverage and Gift Aid. Both matter, but they are downstream of a
prior question: **who is the donor?**

- A **CAF Company Account** is the wrong product. CAF's own wording: "your
  business contributes funds into the account... your contributions into
  the account are considered a charitable donation", and "the company is
  the ultimate donor". For pass-through pledges that misstates the
  accounts, and it KILLS GIFT AID — a company gets corporation-tax relief,
  the charity claims nothing. The 25% uplift disappears.
- The right shape is the **individual** side — a donor-advised structure
  where the guest is the donor, CAF receives and claims Gift Aid on their
  declaration, then grants onward. Same shape as Goodstack Impact
  Foundation and PPGF.
- **CAF Donate is charity-side** — a charity's own donation pages. Not a
  platform disbursement product.
- Whether CAF runs a PLATFORM/partner version of the donor-advised model
  is not visible publicly. That is the question.

**Subject:** Platform partnership — is there a CAF route for donor-chosen giving?

> Hello,
>
> I'm the founder of favpoll (favpoll.com), a UK platform for charitable
> giving at life's moments — memorials, birthdays, retirements. Guests
> pledge small amounts and proceeds go to registered charities the
> organiser chooses. We're a company, not a charity, and we take no
> platform fee.
>
> I've looked at your account range and I don't think any of them is quite
> the fit, so I may be asking for something that sits outside them. A
> Company Account treats the company as the donor — but the money is never
> ours. Each pledge is an individual guest's gift, and we need the donor
> relationship to stay with them so Gift Aid still works. CAF Donate looks
> to be for a charity's own fundraising rather than a platform paying many
> charities.
>
> What I'm after is closer to the donor-advised model, operated through a
> platform: guests donate, CAF receives and claims Gift Aid on each
> donor's declaration, and grants go on to the charity chosen in that
> person's name.
>
> Three questions:
>
> 1. Does CAF offer a partner or platform arrangement of that kind — where
>    the individual remains the donor and we are the route rather than the
>    giver?
> 2. If so, what proportion of UK registered charities can you deliver
>    funds to, and how long for one you haven't paid before?
> 3. Can a grant carry an attribution ("in memory of...")? In our context a
>    gift arriving anonymously undermines the whole point.
>
> If this is the wrong team, a pointer is just as useful. Happy to show a
> live demo.
>
> Thanks,

## 4. Fundraising Regulator — guidance request

**Added 27 Aug.** This one goes FIRST, before the solicitor and before the
first charity, because its answer decides what we are asking either of them
for.

The question is whether the written agreement with a charity has to be in
place **before donations are solicited** or only **before funds are paid
over**. On the strict reading a favpoll cannot publish until its charity has
signed — which kills the memorial case outright, since a family arranging a
funeral for Thursday cannot wait on a hospice finance team. On the looser
reading the hybrid works: publish immediately, onboard during the poll.

Be straight about the optional guest contribution. favpoll takes nothing from
donations, but it does earn from contributions and, later, printed
stationery — and whether that counts as "reward" is the hinge of the whole
answer. Concealing it produces a reply that is worthless.

Expect "we cannot give legal advice". Questions 3 and 4 are asked in terms
they CAN answer regardless, and question 4 is worth having anyway: appearing
on their register is exactly the credential that reassures a charity finance
team on a cold approach.

**To:** the Fundraising Regulator's general enquiries address
**Subject:** Guidance: does a platform taking no fee need agreements in place before soliciting?

> Hello,
>
> I am the founder of favpoll (favpoll.com), a UK platform due to launch
> shortly, and I would like to get our position right before we take a penny.
> We are Josmo Services Ltd, a company, not a charity.
>
> **How it works.** Someone marking an occasion — most often a funeral — sets
> up a favpoll and chooses a registered charity. Guests pledge small amounts.
> We deduct **nothing** from those pledges: 100% of a pledge goes to the
> charity. Card processing is charged separately to the donor at checkout,
> shown to them as its own line, and we retain none of it. Our own income is
> an optional contribution a guest may add on top of their pledge, and in
> future printed stationery. We intend to collect as agent for the charity and
> to pass Gift Aid declarations to them to claim, rather than claiming
> anything ourselves. Funds reach the charity by bank transfer within 10
> working days of a favpoll closing.
>
> **My questions:**
>
> 1. Given we take nothing from donations, and the only sum we keep is an
>    optional contribution the donor chooses to add, would you regard favpoll
>    as a professional fundraiser and/or a commercial participator under Part
>    II of the Charities Act 1992?
>
> 2. If so, does the written agreement under s.59 need to be in place before
>    donations are **solicited**, or before funds are **paid over**? This is
>    the question that shapes our product. A family arranging a funeral often
>    has days, not weeks, and if a favpoll cannot go live until the charity's
>    finance team has signed, most bereaved families will not be able to use
>    it at all. If the agreement is needed only before payment, we would
>    publish immediately and complete the paperwork while the favpoll runs,
>    refunding every guest in full if we could not conclude it.
>
> 3. What solicitation statement would you expect us to make to donors, given
>    our deduction from their pledge is nil, and the only amounts alongside it
>    are the card fee (which goes to the payment provider) and a contribution
>    they opt into?
>
> 4. Can a company in our position register with the Fundraising Regulator?
>    We would like to, and we expect charities to check.
>
> I am happy to talk it through on a call, or to send a link to a working
> demonstration if that is easier than a description.
>
> Thanks for your time,
>
> Joseph Moffatt
> Founder, favpoll — favpoll.com
