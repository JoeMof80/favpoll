# JustGiving — assessment & favpoll positioning (2026-07-29)

Founder ask: where is JustGiving weak, and what are favpoll's honest
advantages? Written after the share-button borrow (#414); facts checked
against JustGiving's own fee pages, July 2026.

## Their model, accurately

- **0% platform fee since 2019**, funded by optional donor tips — the
  same model as favpoll. Do NOT claim a platform-fee advantage; there
  isn't one.
- **Processing 1.9% + 30p** (UK cards) — cheaper than favpoll's Stripe
  retail rate. Do not fight on processing either.
- **5% fee on Gift Aid** where JustGiving acts as Gift Aid agent — a
  real, quotable weakness IF the Goodstack rail passes the uplift at no
  fee (the open Ethan-call question; do not use this line until
  confirmed).
- **Charities pay subscriptions** for the full product (two tiers).
  favpoll charges charities nothing.
- Owned by Blackbaud; enormous brand trust; the default for
  challenge/sponsorship fundraising (marathons, head-shaves).

## Where they are genuinely weak

1. **The page is a transaction, not an experience.** Read story →
   donate → leave a message → leave. Nothing happens for the donor
   beyond the receipt; nothing happens BETWEEN donors. The supporter
   wall is the only social surface and it's one-way.
2. **Nothing happens in the room.** A JustGiving memorial page is an
   online artefact; the gathering itself — the wake, the service, the
   party — has no JustGiving moment. Joy's field data: QR codes on
   orders of service are normalising, donation boxes are dying, and
   JustGiving skews to younger memorials.
3. **Template sameness.** Every page is the same page. The person being
   remembered is a photo and a story field.
4. **Participation is writing.** The only way a donor expresses
   themselves is composing a message — which many people, especially
   older guests at a memorial, find hard. Picking a favourite biscuit
   is answerable by everyone in the room in five seconds.
5. **The data dies on the page.** A closed JustGiving page is a
   gravestone URL. Nothing accumulates.

## favpoll's honest advantages (the wedge)

- **Giving as a shared activity, not an individual transaction.** The
  guest gives, casts their own favourite, receives the reveal (a gift,
  not a gate), and watches the standing move live with everyone else in
  the room. JustGiving monetises sympathy; favpoll creates a moment.
- **Built for the gathering**: QR on the order of service, the live
  display at the wake, the printable pack. This is the ground
  JustGiving structurally cannot hold — their product has no in-room
  surface at all.
- **Answerable by everyone** — the favourite question includes the
  8-year-old and the 88-year-old; the shared fund includes the guest
  with no money. Participation isn't gated on eloquence or a card.
- **Up to 3 charities, split equally** — JustGiving pages are
  single-beneficiary.
- **Free for charities** — no subscription tier for the full product.
- **The record** — every pledge feeds a permanent, growing public
  artefact. JustGiving accumulates nothing a donor can point to.

## Positioning line (internal)

Do NOT position as "a better JustGiving" — they own intent-driven
sponsorship (someone runs a marathon, you sponsor them from your sofa)
and we should not fight for it; Ben's Channel Swim-style fundraisers
are our *adjacent* case, not our beachhead. favpoll's ground is **the
gathering**: the moments when the givers are in a room together and
the giving should feel like part of the occasion.

> JustGiving asks you to give. favpoll asks you to take part.

For celebrant/FD conversations: "the donation box is dying and the
JustGiving link skews young — favpoll is the QR on the order of
service that the whole room can answer."
