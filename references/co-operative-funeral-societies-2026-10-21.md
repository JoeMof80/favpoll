# The co-operative funeral channel — sector map and approach

**Researched 21 October 2026.** Approach copy lives in
`references/funeral-society-approaches-2026-10-21.md`. Everything below is either sourced (links at
the end) or marked as an estimate. Section 8 separates the two, because these

## 1. What PPGF actually requires

**About £10k of donations a year, and a credible volume story.** From Joe
Metcalfe, who ran a PPGF integration at Thrift+:

> *"I do think they aim for something like **£10k per year**. We were
> relatively established but not very."*

> *"There will be a process of explaining who you are and **that there are a
> significant volume of donations expected**."*

**£10k a year is about 25 funerals at £400.** A single independent funeral
director conducts roughly 100 a year, so **Max Kelly's five-person firm in
Nantwich clears it on its own**, and Palins plus Tresidders clear it several
times over.

### The $10m line on their form is not a bar

PPGF's "Become a Partner" survey says *"we are particularly interested in
hearing from you if you operate one of the following, and raise more than $10m
for charity each year."* That is who they court, not who they take:

- The survey's own Q6 offers a **"£0 – £1,000,000"** band, so submissions far
  below are expected.
- **Thrift+ is the existence proof** — a small UK company, nowhere near $10m,
  integrated successfully, with PPGF *"brilliantly supportive"* and *"no
  concerns"*.
- PPGF's partner roster is PayPal itself, eBay for Charity, GoFundMe, Humble
  Bundle, Uber, Twitch, Meta, Deed and Nextdoor — and PayPal's newsroom puts
  the entire programme at *"more than $35 million every year"*. **$10m is
  roughly a third of PPGF's whole annual programme.** If it were an
  eligibility bar, PPGF would have about three partners.

### So what is this document for

**The society channel is not a prerequisite for PPGF.** For the application,
the two funeral directors already in conversation plus the sector context here
are enough; the only thing blocking it is a reply, and the chase date was
20 October 2026.

Read sections 3–5 as **the volume story** — the thing that makes "a
significant volume of donations expected" defensible — and as a growth plan
worth running on its own merits.

## 2. "Co-op Funeralcare" is not one company

There are three tiers, and they behave completely differently.

### Tier 1 — The Co-operative Group (not reachable)

Funeral Services Limited, trading as Co-op Funeralcare, is a **wholly owned
subsidiary** of the Co-operative Group. ~1,000 homes, ~93,000 funerals a year,
employed staff, Manchester head office.

- **These are not franchises.** A branch manager has no procurement authority
  whatever. A good relationship with a local branch filters nowhere.
- Some branches still trade under acquired family names. Still centrally owned.
- **The Crewe homes (32 Mill Street; Middlewich Street CW1 4EA) are Group.**
  Their branch pages sit on `coop.co.uk`, which is the tell — every
  independent society has its own domain. There is no Co-op funeral home in
  Nantwich itself.

### Tier 2 — Independent co-operative societies (the targets)

Separate legal entities with their own boards and chief executives, licensed
to use the brand or trading under their own. Southern Co-op states it plainly:
*"an independent co-operative, although we are licensed to use the national
Co-operative Funeralcare branding."*

**One decision covers dozens of homes. The decision-maker has a name.**

### Tier 3 — Independent family firms

~4,000 of them, plus SAIF and NAFD membership. Fast, no procurement, but one
firm per conversation. Already in play: Gavin Palin Funeral Services
(Nantwich) and Tresidders / Crewe & Nantwich Funeral Service — both emailed
October 2026 via Joy Bratherton's referral, no reply yet.

For this tier the "filters through everything" route is not a firm at all —
it is **SAIF or NAFD**. One relationship, many firms.

## 4. The volume story

Only OurCoop publishes volumes, and that gives the conversion rate for
everyone else:

> **16,966 arrangements across 150+ homes ≈ 113 funerals per home per year**,
> which squares with the Group's ~93 per home.

Applying ~100–113 per home to the rest:

| | Homes | Est. funerals/yr |
|---|---|---|
| OurCoop | 150+ | 16,966 *(published, pre-Midcounties)* |
| East of England Co-op | ~60 | ~6,000–6,800 |
| Scotmid | 40 | ~4,000–4,500 |
| Lincolnshire Co-op | ~20 | ~2,000–2,300 |
| Heart of England Co-op | ~6 | ~600–700 |
| **Independent sector total** | | **~30,000–38,000** |

At **£400 per collection**, that is **£12m – £15.2m of in-memory giving a
year** passing through the independent co-operative sector.

**The headline for an application: a defined, named, finite sector conducting
30–38,000 funerals a year, reachable in five or six conversations.** The point
is not the size of the number — it is that the flow already exists, is
measurable, and the route to it can be named person by person.

No discount is applied for direct cremations. They are 21% of UK funerals and
have no service, so a collection basket cannot serve them — but a favpoll is a
page, and does not need a gathering. The uncertainty they introduce is in the
**per-favpoll** figure, not in the size of the base: a direct-cremation family
passes the link round itself rather than having an order of service carry it.
See `references/ppgf-volume-note-2026-10.md`.

### The precedent that makes this winnable

Under a heading **"Memorialisation and sustainability"**, their own report says:

> *"Our partnership with **The Lovely Gift Group** continues to provide
> families with a curated online range of personalised memorial items,
> including seasonal tributes, condolence gifts and memorial items for
> children and pets. Many items can be personalised with names, messages and
> photographs, helping families create meaningful ways to remember loved
> ones."*

A third-party, commercial, family-facing digital add-on around remembrance.
**That is favpoll's slot, described in their words, in their own report.**

**And it is documented well beyond the report.** In December 2024 Central
Co-op funeralcare launched the store with the Lovely Gift Group at
**memorialisationgifts.co.uk**, covered by Co-operative News and Funeral
Service Times, and it sits on their own site under Additional Services →
Memorialisation. Two details matter more than the partnership itself:

- They describe it as **"the first fully transactional online platform for such
  products to be offered by a funeralcare business."** They take pride in being
  first to do a transactional online thing in funeralcare. favpoll is a
  transactional online thing. **This is the strongest single line to aim at
  them**, and it is their own.
- Lovely Gift Group's founder, **Helen Davies**, says the two have been working
  together **since 2021** — a four-year relationship, not an experiment. So the
  question is not whether they will work with an outside product, but on what
  terms.
- **The deal was done by the Head of Funeral.** The December 2024 announcement
  quotes **Lee Bevan, head of funeral** at Central Co-op, alongside Davies —
  not a procurement function and not a C-suite executive. That is the role
  Glenn Barsby holds now, which confirms he is the decision-maker for this kind
  of partnership rather than a step towards one. (Bevan held the title in
  December 2024 and Barsby holds it in 2026, so it changed hands in between —
  the same churn as the executive layer.) Commercial terms were not disclosed
  in either trade report.

**There is a second precedent too: the Planting Promise.** In partnership with
the **Wildlife and Environmental Society of Malawi** they plant a tree for
every funeral they conduct — **17,653 trees in 2025**. A third-party
charitable tie-in applied to every single funeral, which is structurally
closer to favpoll than the gift store is. It also independently corroborates
the funeral volume (17,653 trees against 16,966 arrangements; different
periods, same order of magnitude).

So favpoll would be the third outside partner in this space, not the first.

Two sentences earlier they state the direction themselves:

> *"Whether providing traditional funerals, sustainable options or **new ways
> to remember loved ones**, our mission is always the same..."*

### The pitch, and note that it is not a software pitch

16,966 arrangements × £400 = **£6.8m of in-memory giving passing through their
funeral homes a year.**

Set that against **OurCoop's own £802k of reported community investment**: the
flow is five to eight times larger, and it appears **nowhere in 78 pages**,
because they do not capture it. The report contains no mention of in-memory
giving or charitable collections at funerals at all.

> **The pitch: you are a democratically-owned society that reports £802k of
> community investment to Members, and meanwhile several million pounds a year
> of your families' charitable giving passes through your homes uncounted. We
> make it visible, and we never touch it.**

That is a co-operative argument, not a technology one. No funeral director has
ever opened a conversation with them that way.

### The plumbing

They run their own funeral management system, **"Our Care Hub"**, rolled out
to the Chelmsford Star homes this year. So integration means a link from a
system they already own rather than new software — and there is an
identifiable technology owner in Selina Butterfield-Mashoofi.

### The risks, honestly

- **Integration year.** Heads down, cost focus, nine months into merging three
  societies.
- **A shrinking business.** Arrangements down 5.4%, their own words citing
  "declining market share". Cuts both ways: pressure makes a free
  differentiator attractive, and pressure also kills projects.
- **The standing gate.** They will ask "which charities?" within two minutes.

### What favpoll can offer them, and what it cannot

The Lovely Gift arrangement is almost certainly commission on products sold —
a transactional store, supplier-run, referrals driven by the funeral homes,
OurCoop taking margin. Terms were never published, so this is inference, but it
is the obvious shape.

**That is the problem to prepare for: favpoll has no commission to share, by
design.** It takes nothing from a pledge, and that invariant is the entire
basis of the pitch to charities and families.

> **EXPECT THEM TO ASK FOR A CUT OF DONATIONS**, because that is their existing
> mental model for a partner in this space. The answer has to be rehearsed
> before the question arrives: **nothing ever comes off a pledge.** Conceding
> it once destroys the proposition, and it would likely make favpoll a
> commercial participator under Part II of the Charities Act 1992 — see
> `references/legal-brief-the-fork-2026-10.md`.

Four things to offer, in this order.

#### 1. THE MEMBER PERK, AND IT IS NOT ABOUT FUNERALS — lead with this

**OurCoop is overwhelmingly not a funeral business.** 150+ funeral homes
against 173+ food stores, plus travel, childcare and utilities, and **over a
million Members**, almost none of whom will arrange a funeral this year. A
funeral-only perk is worthless to 99% of them.

So the favpoll member benefit belongs **in the food stores, not the funeral
homes**: a discount on a favpoll keepsake or print pack for *any* occasion — a
birthday, a wedding, a charity run. A benefit a million Members can use.

**And it answers something their own Annual Report asks for**, which is why
this is the strongest card in the hand:

> *"The creation of OurCoop provides significant opportunities to raise
> awareness of the Funeral business among Members and customers who are
> transacting with the other co-operative businesses operating across the new
> Society."*

They have written down that they want cross-business traffic into funeral. A
favpoll perk in the food aisles does exactly that: a Member uses favpoll for a
40th, and FuneralCoop is the name on it. **It also does not compete for Glenn
Barsby's attention during an integration year**, because it is not a funeral
project.

Note what makes it a *benefit*: a favpoll is free to everyone, so favpoll
itself can never be a member benefit — a benefit is something Members get that
non-members do not. Every item on OurCoop's own list is attached to a purchase:
a bouquet with any floral tribute, engraving on an ashes casket, the first 50
letters paid for on a headstone, a reward redeemable in their stores. **So the
perk is a discount on merchandise**, which mirrors "the first 50 letters paid
for" exactly. It costs favpoll margin rather than cash, and only on real sales.

#### 2. Merchandise — the keepsake is the first SKU, and the insert card is free

**The insert card is not a product, and should not be sold.** Its QR is unique
per favpoll, so it cannot be mass pre-printed, which kills any trade-supply
model: there is no box of cards to sell a funeral director. What remains is the
director printing it free from favpoll, favpoll printing and posting inside a
5–10 day funeral window at thin margin, or their existing order-of-service
printer running it alongside for nothing. **Its value is distribution, not
revenue** — it is what puts the link in fifty pairs of hands. Give it away.

**The product is the keepsake.** `apps/web/components/keepsake/` already holds
it (keepsake-sheet, keepsake-document, keepsake-view, image and CSV export).
Bought by the family *after* the event: no deadline to miss, emotionally
valuable, worth printing properly — and **precisely what Lovely Gift sells**,
personalised memorial items bought after a funeral. That is the real analogue,
and it becomes the first piece of favpoll merchandise under the "one hero SKU
plus the print pack" direction recorded in `references/PROJECT.md`.

**What making it a product involves:** a price and a margin; a print-and-post
partner or print-on-demand API; packaging and postage; a complaints and reprint
policy; VAT (personalised stationery is standard-rated, unlike books and
leaflets — check it, it moves the margin); and a goods checkout, which is a
different Stripe flow from a pledge with different consumer-rights treatment.
Weeks of work, not days.

**CO-BRANDED STATIONERY WITH A GENERIC QR IS A PRODUCT — founder,
21 October 2026, and it is the missing object.** The insert card fails as a
product because its QR is unique per favpoll. A card whose QR points at
**favpoll.com/memorial** (the register landing page, already built — not the
home page, which also covers birthdays) has no per-favpoll uniqueness, so it
**can** be mass printed, and because it carries no person's name, co-branding
it is appropriate.

That gives three distinct objects, and conflating them is what caused the
confusion:

| | Object | QR points to | Printed | Branding | Commercial |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Acquisition** | Co-branded card or leaflet, given to the family by the arranger | `/memorial`, partner-attributed | **Mass, in bulk** | **Co-branded — correct here** | **A product**: trade supply |
| **Participation** | Insert card, handed out at the service | That one favpoll | Per funeral, by them or the family | Firm's **name in words** only | **Free** — it is distribution |
| **Retention** | Keepsake, bought after the event | — | On demand | **The person's name only** | The paid SKU |

**The strongest form of the acquisition object is the "in lieu of flowers"
card**, which is already the recorded insight in `references/PROJECT.md`
("'in lieu of flowers' cards double as funeral-director channel marketing").
It works because it is an object **the family needs anyway** — they are already
telling people donations rather than flowers. The QR is not favpoll marketing
bolted on; it is the family's own message with a route attached. Other forms: a
counter card for the arranger's desk, or a leaflet in the folder the family
takes home.

**Be honest about the money.** Printed leaflets at trade price are hundreds of
pounds, not thousands — **small revenue, large distribution value.** What it
really buys is an object that justifies a commercial conversation, attribution
that feeds the reporting offer, and a co-operative advertising favpoll at its
own expense, which is the one thing favpoll cannot buy. One upside worth
checking: UK **leaflets and brochures are zero-rated** for VAT where
personalised stationery is standard-rated, so the margin may be better here
than on the keepsake.

**The co-branding rule (founder, 21 October 2026).** A personal artefact
carries **only the person's name**. A keepsake is a family's memento, not a
sponsor's: two logos on it make it a joint venture's product rather than a
record of a life, and favpoll's own mark is already deliberately restrained
there. The **functional** object may carry the firm's name — the insert card's
"Arranged with [firm]", a name in words rather than a logo — because it is
handed out at the service and the family benefits from knowing who arranged it.
**Co-branding belongs on generic merchandise only**, where it commemorates a
collaboration rather than a person: a conversation card deck, or beermats for a
celebration. A **limited edition** is a legitimate reason to co-brand; a
keepsake never is.

On register fit: beermats are wrong for a funeral and good for a 40th or a
wedding, where a favourite and a QR on every table is strong distribution and
the occasion tolerates it. Celebration-register merchandise, not funeral.

#### 3. Aggregate reporting — what co-operative governance rewards

favpoll knows every pledge. If favpolls are created through their funeral homes
or by their Members, favpoll can report **in aggregate**: this many families
used it, this much was raised, to these charities. **They cannot know this
today** — the money never touches them and nobody counts baskets.

Why it is currency: they publish a community-investment figure to Members every
year (**£802k** last year). *"Members and families we served raised £X for
charity"* is a line for that section, for the AGM and for member
communications. A co-operative board answers to Members, not shareholders, so a
reputational number can outrank a small commission line.

Three limits, stated honestly: **aggregate and anonymised only** — individual
families and donor data are not on offer and never should be; it is not
revenue; and in month one the number is zero, so it is a reason to run a pilot
rather than a reason to sign.

#### 4. Funeral plans — the biggest idea, and the most complicated

Their **growing**, FCA-regulated, higher-margin line: Central Co-op Funeral
Plans, with a new website and colleagues trained to sell. And favpoll already
has the matching concept — **a favpoll specified in a will or letter of
wishes**, where the person writes their own favourites and their own reveal in
advance and an executor creates it when the time comes.

So it is not a service delivered at the funeral, it is **something to sell with
the plan**: on the line they are investing in rather than the one in decline,
and it gives plan salespeople something warm to talk about instead of pricing.
It is also favpoll at its most powerful, the person speaking in their own voice
after they are gone.

This is also where the **letter-of-wishes kit** belongs — ranked first for
brand fit in the merchandise direction in `references/PROJECT.md`, and a
pre-need product rather than a post-event one. Note the two product ideas map
onto the two commercial routes: **keepsake → post-event, retail and the member
perk; letter-of-wishes kit → pre-need, the funeral-plan channel.**

**But hold all of this for a second or third conversation.** A pre-paid funeral
plan is a regulated promise and what is "included" in one is regulated. Worse,
a plan may be redeemed in twenty-five years, and favpoll cannot promise a
software feature that far out. It would have to sit **alongside** the plan as a
non-contractual extra, never inside it.

#### Which to lead with

**The member perk, every time.** It is the biggest surface, it costs them
nothing, it needs no procurement, it serves a million Members rather than
17,000 funerals, and it is the one thing their own report says they want. The
keepsake is what makes the perk a perk. Aggregate reporting is the argument
that wins a board rather than a manager. Funeral plans need them to already
believe the first thing works.

## 6. The approach

### Order of operations

1. **One signed charity agreement** — so "where does the money go" has a true
   answer. `references/charity-collection-agreement-DRAFT.md` already has the
   right shape (clause 2 agency, clause 3 no fee, clause 4 the charity claims
   Gift Aid), which is the Code of Fundraising Practice 6.2.1 structure. Needs
   a solicitor, and clause 3 still carries the retired "100% of every pledge"
   claim — settle that before it goes to anyone. **St Luke's Cheshire Hospice**
   is the live prospect; **Alzheimer's Society** (296645) is in the catalogue
   and is the charity in the Palins exemplar. This step needs nobody's
   permission but the charity's.
2. **Funeral-director quotes and a real collection figure.** Palins and
   Tresidders are already emailed. The question that matters most is the third
   one: *what does a collection typically raise at your funerals* — it
   replaces the derived £400 with a number PPGF cannot argue with, and it also
   tests the 150-funerals-per-director figure in the volume note's table.
3. **Approach Glenn Barsby, Head of Funeral at OurCoop. SCHEDULED
   22 October 2026, 10am**, by LinkedIn. Copy and the rules it follows are in
   `references/funeral-society-approaches-2026-10-21.md`. It opens on his Dying
   Matters quote, which is recent, public and verifiable, and it carries **no
   pitch at all** — the point being that the recipient asks for the pitch.
4. **In parallel, find the equivalent named person at East of England Co-op
   and Scotmid.** Together 100 homes and ~10,000 funerals, and a smaller
   society is an easier twenty minutes than OurCoop. **One of them saying yes
   is as useful as OurCoop saying yes, and more likely.**

Steps 1 and 2 are also all that PPGF needs. Nothing here waits on anything
else.

### The ask

**Not adoption, and not a pilot yet.** Twenty minutes, in the register that
worked on Palins — *"I'm a local software engineer... before launching
properly, I'm seeking professional insight to make sure the platform is
appropriate and ethical"* — which puts the person in the expert's chair rather
than the customer's, and carries the pre-launch sequencing without having to
name PPGF.

The pilot is what the conversation is *for*. A society can authorise a pilot
in one locality without procurement.

**What to lead with once asked: the stationery and the live screen.** The
printed insert card that sits with the order of service and carries the firm's
own name, and the favpoll shown on a screen in the room with the result
revealed at the close. Neither is possible without an attended funeral, so
neither can cannibalise the service they sell — they make it bigger. That is a
better offer than removing the admin of counting cash, which is a saving on
something thankless. See `references/ppgf-volume-note-2026-10.md`.

### The channel, and the clock

The founder holds **LinkedIn Premium for one month from about 21 October 2026**,
which is the thing with a deadline on it. InMail reaches someone without
needing a connection accept, gives ~1,900 characters against a connection
request's 300, and removes the silent-failure risk of an inferred email
address. Credits are ~5/month on Career and ~15 on Business, refunded if the
recipient replies within 90 days. Premium search is also how to find the names
at East of England, Scotmid and Lincolnshire. Send inside the month; details in
the approaches file.

### One cheap, legitimate extra

OurCoop is democratically owned — "one Member, one vote", with Membership and
Community Councils. Membership is open and cheap. It gives standing and a real
channel, and it signals that the approach understands the model. Not a
procurement shortcut, and not worth doing cynically; worth doing because it is
true.

### Hospice UK's conference is not the route in

Considered and rejected on 21 October 2026. The **National Conference is
16–18 November 2026 in Liverpool**, but bookings are open only to *"anyone
working for any end of life care provider or related non-profit organisation"*
— **commercial organisations are directed to a separate commercial team.** So
there is no delegate ticket to buy; the prices are exhibitor packages. From
2023–25 pages: Gold £10,000 + VAT, Bronze £4,200–£4,700 + VAT, a "Not For
Profit Exhibitor" package at £1,300 that favpoll would not qualify for, and
newsletter sponsorship at £900–£1,200 + VAT.

**Do not exhibit** — and the reason is sequencing, not cost. Every conversation
on a stand ends at "which charities do you work with?", and until the
collection agreement exists the answer is none. Three better uses of the same
attention:

- **Hospice UK's Commercial Network sign-up is free.** Worth doing now; it also
  surfaces next year's real prices in time to decide properly.
- **The Income Generation Leaders Conference is ~£200 + VAT** (£215
  non-member) — hospice *fundraising* leaders, a far better room than a general
  national conference, at a twentieth of the exhibitor cost. Worth one email to
  ask whether a commercial organisation can attend as a delegate.
- **St Luke's Cheshire Hospice**, properly. One signed agreement from a hospice
  that already met the founder beats two hundred cold hospice conversations,
  and it is what unblocks everything else.

There is a signal in that eligibility rule worth reading: Hospice UK gates the
room to providers and routes vendors to a sales team, which is roughly how an
individual hospice will receive an unsolicited commercial approach. It argues
for entering through a hospice that already knows you and letting them
introduce you.

## 7. The PPGF application

Four moves, none of which require having raised anything:

1. **The flow already exists.** Roughly £100m a year is raised through
   in-memoriam donations managed by UK funeral directors — currently in cash,
   in an envelope, reconciled by hand. favpoll digitises a behaviour that has
   been there for a century. **PPGF are being asked to underwrite capture, not
   demand creation.**
2. **The volume expected is nameable, not hoped for.** 25 funerals covers what
   they look for; two local firms already in conversation cover it several
   times over; and the sector above shows the ceiling — OurCoop (150+ homes),
   East of England (~60), Scotmid (40), then the Group at 93,000.
3. **Cost to serve is near zero.** Thrift+ ran on mass payment files,
   semi-automated, and never built a payments integration (Joe Metcalfe,
   October 2026). PPGF is a small team, and effort on their side is the real
   constraint — so showing favpoll needs almost none of it answers the thing
   that actually matters to them.
4. **Funerals is a vertical they do not cover.** Their partner roster is
   marketplaces, streaming, gaming and giving platforms. In-memory giving is
   one of the largest and least digitised charitable flows in the UK and is
   not on that list. **A new category is a reason to talk, not another me-too
   giving platform.**

## 8. What is solid and what is assumed

### Solid — sourced, quotable

- PPGF's practical requirement of **~£10k/year** (Joe Metcalfe, who ran a PPGF
  integration at Thrift+), and that Thrift+ integrated successfully nowhere
  near $10m.
- The $10m survey wording, the "£0 – £1,000,000" band on the same form, and
  that PPGF's whole programme is "more than $35 million every year" — so $10m
  is a courting preference, not a bar.
- One contract with PPGF, no direct charity relationships needed, but PPGF are
  strict on the wording in T&Cs and on the donation page (Joe Metcalfe).
- Co-op Funeralcare is a wholly owned subsidiary, not a franchise network.
- OurCoop's formation date, constituent societies, registered office and
  society number.
- OurCoop funeral figures: 16,966 arrangements, £64.4m, 983 colleagues,
  £802k community investment, 150+ homes.
- The Lovely Gift Group partnership and the "new ways to remember loved ones"
  line — verbatim from their Annual Report 2026, and independently documented:
  the December 2024 launch of memorialisationgifts.co.uk, the "first fully
  transactional online platform" claim, and Helen Davies' "since 2021".
- The Planting Promise with the Wildlife and Environmental Society of Malawi,
  17,653 trees in 2025, one per funeral conducted.
- Glenn Barsby as Head of Funeral, from two independent sources (Membership
  Matters 5 May 2026; Funeral Service Times July 2026).
- Sara Dunham's departure at the end of September 2026, announced 8 May 2026;
  Andy Peake's departure from the role on 25 January 2026.
- Southern Co-op → Siena Co-operative Limited, 27 July 2026, CMA pending.
- Home counts for East of England (~60), Scotmid (40), Southern (69).
- **Direct cremations are 21% of UK funerals**, average £1,628 against £3,828
  for a simple attended funeral and £4,510 for a traditional one (SunLife Cost
  of Dying 2026).
- The published **average in-memory donation of ~£38**, and Marie Curie
  receiving ~£1m a year from funeral-service donations.
- Hospice UK National Conference dates and its commercial-organisation
  eligibility rule.

### Assumed — do not state as fact

- **£400 per funeral collection.** Derived in
  `references/ppgf-volume-note-2026-10.md` from three labelled assumptions:
  **50 mourners × 40% participation × £20 average pledge**. The £20 was taken
  as the mid-point of a published £10–£30 per-mourner range.
  **Independent corroboration found 21 October 2026: the published average
  in-memory donation is ~£38** (funeral-notices.co.uk), and Marie Curie alone
  receives ~£1m a year from funeral-service donations — so £20 a pledge is
  conservative by roughly half, and the note's claim to be "deliberately
  modest" now has evidence behind it rather than just intent.
  Still an estimate until a funeral director gives a real figure, and **the
  single most load-bearing unknown in the whole plan.**
- **That the £400 unit holds for direct cremations.** It is anchored on 50
  mourners at a service; a direct-cremation family distributes the link itself,
  and the reachable number is unknown in either direction.
- **~100–113 funerals per home per year** for societies that do not publish —
  derived from OurCoop and the Group.
- **The ~£100m UK in-memoriam total.** An "industry estimate" published by
  InMemory / donateinmemory.co.uk, **a vendor selling funeral-donation
  software**, with no primary source. Hedge it or find better before putting it
  in writing — a partnerships team that checks it and finds a competitor's
  marketing page is a bad first impression.
- **`firstname.lastname@centralengland.coop`** — inferred from one published
  address.
- Lincolnshire Co-op's home count: sources say 19 and 22.
