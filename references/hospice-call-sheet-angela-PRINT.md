## The two lines you do not cross

Getting these wrong marks you as not understanding their world, and there
is no recovering it in the same meeting.

1. **Nobody approaches a family before a bereavement.** Never suggest it,
   never imply the product could. "Families soon to lose someone" is not a
   market, it is a door you do not knock on.
2. **The clinical and family-support side never raises money.** Do not
   propose anything that puts a donation ask near care.

The correct shape, which you already know: families come to the hospice
ALREADY asking *"we'd like donations to St Luke's — how?"*, usually via
the funeral director. The hospice answers with its in-memory menu. **You
are asking to be on that menu.** The hospice never initiates.

and it opens the relationship on their terms.

## Facts at your fingertips

**What it is**

- Guests **pledge to a charity** and share their own favourite; the
  person's own favourite is revealed to them after they pledge.
- **No fee — 100% of every pledge reaches the charity.** favpoll runs on
  optional tips, collected separately from the donation.
- **Registered UK charities only** — never funeral costs, never personal
  funds.
- Guests need **no account** — email only; card, Apple Pay or Google Pay.
- A shared fund can quietly cover guests who can't pay.
- It can be a whisper or a centrepiece: cards on tables with a QR code,
  or a live display in the room. The family chooses.

**What is NOT live yet — say it before you're asked**

- **Gift Aid is not wired.** Do not imply it is. It is coming; it is not
  here.
- **Disbursement is manual for the first few** — a favpoll closes, the
  transfer is made by hand. Entirely workable at this volume, and honest.
- Still pre-launch. A first favpoll would be exactly that — a first.

Being straight about these is worth more than looking finished. This
sector can smell a platform overselling itself, and they have all been
pitched before.
