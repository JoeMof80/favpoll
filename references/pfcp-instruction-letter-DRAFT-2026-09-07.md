Dear [name],

I run favpoll (favpoll.com), a UK platform through which guests at an
occasion — a memorial, a birthday, a fundraiser — pledge money to a
registered charity chosen by the organiser. I am seeking a scoped,
fixed-fee written opinion on our status under Part II of the Charities
Act 1992 and the Charitable Institutions (Fund-Raising) Regulations
1994, and on what that status requires of us operationally. The
Fundraising Regulator has told us (by phone, 1 September 2026) that
this classification turns on legal advice they cannot provide.

**Who we are.** favpoll is a trading name of Josmo Services Limited, a
UK private limited company. The platform went live at favpoll.com on
7 September 2026. Volumes are currently minimal (founder testing);
we want this opinion before soliciting the public at scale.

**How the money works (current, accurate as of 7 September 2026):**

- An organiser creates a page and names a registered charity as the
  beneficiary. Guests pledge amounts to it.
- The guest's card is charged exactly their pledge plus any optional
  tip they choose to add for favpoll. We take no platform fee.
- Card processing costs are absorbed at settlement: the charity
  receives the pledge minus its share of card processing (roughly
  1.5% + 20p per transaction). This replaced an earlier model in
  which the processing fee was charged to the guest on top; we
  changed it so the guest's total is exactly what they choose.
- The optional tip is favpoll's only income from donations. It is
  presented as optional, separately, and never taken from the pledge.
- Funds are held in Josmo's Stripe account and remitted to the
  charity by bank transfer after the page closes (target: within 10
  working days). No disbursement intermediary is currently
  contracted; remittance is direct and manual at present volumes.
- We intend to collect as agent for the charity under a written
  agreement, and to pass any Gift Aid declarations to the charity to
  claim itself; we claim nothing.

**The questions we would like answered in writing:**

1. **Status.** Is Josmo Services Ltd, operating favpoll as described,
   a "professional fundraiser" and/or a "commercial participator"
   under Part II of the 1992 Act and the 1994 Regulations — noting
   that our income is a donor-optional tip rather than a fee or
   deduction, but that the charity's receipt is net of its card
   processing share?

2. **If either status applies.** What must our written agreement with
   each charity contain to satisfy s.59 and the Regulations (we hold
   a working draft), and what solicitation statement must we make to
   donors under s.60 — in particular, how to state accurately a
   deduction that is limited to third-party card processing costs?

3. **If neither applies.** Does anything else in the 1992 Act, the
   Regulations, or general law (including trust or agency principles
   over the held funds) constrain how we solicit, hold, or transmit
   these donations?

4. **Sequencing scenarios.** We would like the opinion to address
   three operating postures explicitly, so we can build the product
   to the compliant one:

   a. **Consent-first.** An organiser may create a page naming any
      registered charity (sourced from the Charity Commission
      register), but pledging is disabled until the charity has
      agreed in writing; pre-consent, the page states that pledges
      open when the charity confirms. Is the pre-consent display of
      the charity's name (and/or logo and registered number) itself a
      solicitation or otherwise objectionable, and if so what
      presentation would be acceptable?

   b. **Collect-then-agree.** Pledges are collected for a charity we
      have not yet contacted; we hold the funds, approach the charity
      at or before first remittance, and enter the written agreement
      then. If the charity accepts, funds transfer and the charity is
      onboarded for future pages; if it declines, it is delisted. Is
      this lawful for a first occasion, and under what conditions
      (segregation or trust over held funds, remittance deadlines,
      donor statements, refund obligations if the charity declines)?

   c. **Refusal handling.** Where a charity declines or does not
      respond: what must happen to funds already held, and within
      what period?

5. **Registration.** The Fundraising Regulator has indicated
   non-charity registration is possible for us. Is there any reason
   not to register, and does registration bear on any of the above?

We can provide: the live product (favpoll.com), the exact checkout
copy, our draft charity agreement, and the Fundraising Regulator
correspondence. We would welcome a fixed-fee quote for a written
opinion covering the above; we are a small early-stage company and
scope discipline matters to us.
