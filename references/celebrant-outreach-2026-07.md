# Celebrant outreach — Joy (2026-07)

Discovery conversation with the celebrant who led the founder's mother's
memorial service (~2024). Rationale: celebrants sit closer to favpoll's
heart than funeral directors — they are the professional who sits with
the family and asks "what did she love?", which is favpoll's exact
question. Personal connection means honest answers, not polite ones.
Sensitivity handled by candour: favpoll partly grew out of that
service, and the text says so.

## Sent text (2026-07-23, SMS — first touch; call to follow)

> Hi Joy, my name is Joe Moffatt - you led the service for my mum,
> Margaret, two years ago. I'm a web engineer, and I've been building
> an app for guests at life events to donate to charity - an idea
> partly inspired from that day. Before launch, I'm seeking
> professional insight to make sure the app is appropriate and
> ethical. Would you be kind enough to spare 20 minutes for a call in
> the next week or two?

The "appropriate and ethical" framing is deliberate (founder's line):
it casts her as a guardian of the register, not a sales target.

**Follow-up plan:** one gentle text nudge after ~a week of silence;
after that a direct call is fair follow-through.

## Question sheet for the call

Craft: ask everything BEFORE showing the product — once she's seen it,
answers become kindness. Ask about recent concrete services, not
generalities ("of your last ten…").

1. **Donations baseline** — of your last ten services, how many had a
   charitable collection? How was it actually done (plate, funeral
   director's page, MuchLoved link in the order of service)? What goes
   wrong with the current ways?
2. **Funeral costs** — how often do collections go toward costs rather
   than charity? (Probes the boundary favpoll deliberately won't
   cross: charity only, never costs.)
3. **The money register** — what amounts feel normal; where is the
   line where anything about money feels wrong in that room? Is a
   pre-selected tip inappropriate? (favpoll currently pre-selects the
   middle tip option — her reaction IS the data.)
4. **The reveal test** — describe the mechanic (guests pledge, share
   their own favourite, then hers is revealed to them) and watch her
   face. Gift or gimmick, in the memorial register?
5. **Service driven by the poll** — could the winning hymn be the one
   sung? When does the order of service lock; would guest input before
   the day feel like participation or pressure?
6. **Who organises, and when** — in the blur between death and
   service, who realistically sets this up (family / celebrant /
   funeral director), and when: before the service or the weeks after?
7. **Family struggles** — what do families find hardest when choosing
   hymns, readings, music? Does the "favourite" frame help or add
   pressure?
8. **Incumbents from the room's view** — MuchLoved, Memory Giving,
   JustGiving pages: who chooses them and why?
9. **What she'd need to recommend a platform** — trust markers, fee
   clarity, knowing who's behind it. (The celebrant channel quietly
   scoping itself.)
10. **The will angle** — do families arrive with wishes the person
    left about hymns or charities? (Letter-of-wishes thesis.)
11. **If it goes well** — would she be open to showing it to a
    suitable family, no obligation? One warm pilot through a celebrant
    beats fifty cold sign-ups.
